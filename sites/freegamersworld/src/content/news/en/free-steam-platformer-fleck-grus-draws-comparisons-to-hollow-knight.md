A new free-to-play platformer on Steam is picking up praise for its polished movement and evocative art style, with players likening its look to that of Hollow Knight. Fleck & Grus, developed by students at DigiPen Europe-Bilbao, currently holds an 88% positive rating on the platform.

## What the Game Is About

Unlike Hollow Knight, Fleck & Grus is not a metroidvania and largely avoids combat, focusing instead on precision platforming through tightly designed levels. According to the game's own description, players take on the role of Grus, tasked with escorting a companion named Fleck safely back to his hometown before nightfall. The setup involves Fleck being the son of Atlantis, with the story hinging on a giant kraken threatening to flood the world if the pair fail to reach the city in time.

The journey takes players through a range of biomes, including swamps and the underwater city of Atlantis itself, with the pace built around bouncing mechanics, tricky jumps, and environmental hazards. While combat is minimal overall, the game does include a handful of set-piece encounters, culminating in a confrontation with the kraken.

## Player Reception

Steam reviewers have responded favorably to the game's visuals and controls. One player praised the art direction and overall feel, calling the game "awesome," while another highlighted its potential as a speedrunning title, drawing comparisons to Super Mario Odyssey and Celeste due to its fluid movement and route-optimization opportunities. That same reviewer noted some initial awkwardness with the controls, such as holding a button to parry, but still rated the experience positively given its free price tag and short length.

Most players report finishing Fleck & Grus in around three hours, making it a compact but apparently well-crafted addition to Steam's library of free titles.

## A Student Project

Fleck & Grus stands out partly because of its origins: it was created by a group of students rather than an established studio. For players interested in supporting emerging developers, downloading and trying the game costs nothing, and its reception so far suggests it delivers a level of visual and mechanical polish above what might be expected from a student project.

The release arrives at a moment when many gamers are bracing for a wave of expensive AAA titles later this year, including Grand Theft Auto VI, Silent Hill Townfall, and Call of Duty: Modern Warfare VI, among others. Free offerings like Fleck & Grus provide an alternative for players looking to fill the gap without spending money, and Steam continues to host a steady stream of similar no-cost releases for those seeking budget-friendly entertainment.

Apple is preparing to launch a redesigned iPad mini later this year that would mark the first time an iPad has offered official water resistance, according to a report from Bloomberg's Mark Gurman. The tablet is reportedly on track to arrive by October.

No iPad currently carries an IP rating, and Apple advises against exposing existing models to moisture. To change that, Apple has reportedly developed a vibration-based speaker system that does away with traditional speaker holes. Rather than pushing audio through visible grilles, sound would be produced by vibrating flat sections of the tablet's chassis, effectively turning parts of the body into a speaker diaphragm. This differs from the approach Apple uses on the iPhone, which achieves its IP68 rating through adhesives and gaskets sealing existing openings. Removing speaker holes altogether would close off one of the main pathways through which water and dust typically enter a device.

Apple has explored this kind of acoustic engineering before; a 2014 patent described a "mechanically actuated panel acoustic system" that generates sound by vibrating flat panels instead of relying on grilles. It is not yet known what specific IP rating the new iPad mini will achieve, or whether it will approach the iPhone's six-meter, 30-minute submersion tolerance.

## Closing the gap with e-readers

A water-resistant design would address a long-standing advantage held by dedicated e-readers such as the Kindle Paperwhite and Kobo Libra Color, both of which are built to survive submersion and are commonly used in baths or by pools. The iPad mini's compact size has made it a popular reading device, but its lack of water protection has kept it at a disadvantage in that specific use case.

## Other expected changes

Water resistance is reportedly just one of several major updates planned for the device. The iPad mini is expected to gain an OLED display, becoming only the second iPad line after the iPad Pro to move away from LCD. This should bring deeper blacks, higher contrast, and improved power efficiency. However, a Korean source has claimed the panel will be an 8.4-inch LTPS display running at 60Hz, suggesting ProMotion will not be included.

Apple is also said to be planning a substantial processor upgrade. Code inadvertently published by Apple pointed to the A19 Pro chip used in the iPhone Air, while separate evidence found in a macOS kernel debug kit suggests the unreleased A20 Pro could be used instead. Either option would be a significant jump from the A17 Pro in the current iPad mini, which launched in October 2024 with Apple Intelligence support.

Reports of mass production of OLED panels for the device by Samsung Display, said to have begun in June, support the expected October timeline. The current iPad mini starts at $599 after Apple's broader price increases in June, and earlier estimates suggested an OLED model could cost up to $100 more, though that figure predates the recent price adjustments. Apple has not confirmed any of these details.
